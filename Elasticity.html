PART ONE

The elasticity of demand

How responsive quantity demanded is to a change in price.

Elasticity of demandDefinition

Elasticity and slope are related, but they are different

Elasticity of demand (Ed)
Measures how responsive quantity demanded is to a change in price. More responsive means more elastic.
Elasticity rule
Starting from the same price and quantity, a flatter demand (or supply) curve is more elastic than a steeper one.
  • Elasticity uses percentage changes, so it does not depend on the units you measure in.
  • Slope uses the raw change in price and quantity.
  • The same price increase barely moves Q on a steep curve and moves it a lot on a flat one.

Elasticity of demandDeterminants

What makes demand more elastic?

  • Ease of finding substitutes: easier to substitute, more elastic.
  • Time to adjust: more time, more substitutes found, more elastic. There are few substitutes for gasoline this month, but over years drivers buy efficient cars or switch fuels.
  • Definition of the commodity: a narrow definition or a specific brand has more substitutes, so it is more elastic.
  • Necessities vs. luxuries: luxuries are more elastic.
  • Share of budget: a larger share of the budget means more elastic.

PART TWO

Calculating the elasticity of demand

Percentage change in quantity divided by percentage change in price, using the midpoint method.

Calculating elasticityThe midpoint method

Use the average as the base

Ed= %ΔQd ÷ %ΔP

Midpoint method
Compute each percentage change using the average of the two values as the base, so the answer is the same whether the price rises or falls.%ΔQ = (Q2 − Q1) ÷ [(Q1 + Q2) / 2]%ΔP = (P2 − P1) ÷ [(P1 + P2) / 2]
Worked example: price drops from $4 to $3; quantity rises from 120 to 150.
  • %ΔQ = (150 − 120) ÷ 135 = +22.22% (base: (120 + 150) / 2 = 135)
  • %ΔP = (3 − 4) ÷ 3.50 = −28.57% (base: (4 + 3) / 2 = 3.50)
  • Ed = 22.22 ÷ (−28.57) = −0.78

PART THREE

Elasticity and total revenue

Revenue = Price × Quantity. Elasticity tells you what happens to revenue when the price changes.

Total revenueThe rule

A price change pulls revenue in two directions

R= P × Q

  • A higher P raises revenue per unit, but Q falls. Elasticity tells you which effect is bigger.
  • Inelastic demand: Q barely falls, so revenue rises when price rises.
  • Elastic demand: Q falls a lot, so revenue falls when price rises.

PART FIVE

The elasticity of supply

How responsive quantity supplied is to a change in price.

Elasticity of supplyDefinition

The same formula, applied to sellers

Es= %ΔQs ÷ %ΔPmidpoint method again

Elasticity of supply (Es)
Measures how responsive quantity supplied is to a change in price. Supply elasticities are positive.
Worked example: price rises from $40 to $50; quantity supplied rises from 100 to 120.
  • %ΔQ = 20 ÷ 110 = +18.2%
  • %ΔP = 10 ÷ 45 = +22.2%
  • Es = 18.2 ÷ 22.2 = 0.82, so supply is inelastic.
Elasticity of supplyDeterminants

How fast do per-unit costs rise with output?

  • The fundamental determinant: if expanding production pushes per-unit costs up steeply, supply is inelastic. If output can grow at roughly constant unit cost, supply is elastic.
  • Share of the input market: supply is more elastic when the industry can expand without bidding up its input prices.
  • Local vs. global: local supply is much more elastic than global supply, because supplies can flow in from next door.
  • Time horizon: supply is more elastic in the long run than in the short run.

PART SIX

Applications of supply elasticity

Gun buybacks and housing markets.

ApplicationsGun buybacks

Why gun buybacks disappoint

  • Several U.S. cities spent millions buying back guns to reduce the number on the street.
  • When police buy guns, that is an increase in demand.
  • The local supply of guns is very elastic, because guns flow in from surrounding areas.
The prediction
With elastic supply, the street price doesn’t rise and the quantity on the street doesn’t fall. The buyback is absorbed by guns flowing in. Economics predicts these programs don’t reduce the number of guns on the street.

Questions to answer without notes

QUESTION 1

Is demand for BMWs more or less elastic than demand for cars in general?

More elastic. Specific brands are more elastic than general categories, and luxuries are more elastic than necessities.

QUESTION 2

Price drops from $4 to $3 and quantity rises from 120 to 150. What is Ed?

22.22% ÷ (−28.57%) = −0.78, so demand is inelastic.

QUESTION 3

An elasticity of 1.27 means demand is…?

Elastic, because 1.27 > 1.

QUESTION 4

Wine has |Ed| = 1.2. The price rises. What happens to industry revenue?

Revenue decreases, because demand is elastic.

QUESTION 5

iPhone demand is inelastic. Supply increases. What happens to revenue?

Revenue decreases. The extra units sold are outweighed by the larger price drop.

QUESTION 6

Is the supply of roofing nails in Fargo, ND elastic or inelastic?

Elastic. Nails are easy to make at constant unit cost, they are a tiny share of the steel market, and local supply is more elastic than global supply.

Takeaway

Summary

Demand

Elasticity of demand measures how responsive quantity demanded is to price. Compute it with the midpoint method.

Revenue

|Ed| < 1: TR and P move together. |Ed| > 1: they move in opposite directions.

Determinants

Substitutes, time, brand vs. category, luxury vs. necessity, and budget share.

Supply

Elasticity of supply depends on how fast per-unit costs rise, and it is higher locally and in the long run.

Applications

Shrinking farm revenue, gun buybacks that don’t work, and why housing booms raise prices in some cities and construction in others.
